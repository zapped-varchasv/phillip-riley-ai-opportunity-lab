# Delivery and handover plan

This is a proposed supervised plan for 12 weeks at 15–19 hours per week, approximately 180–228 hours total. It prioritises discovery, small trials and commercial recommendations over a production software build.

| Weeks | Focus | Tangible output | Decision owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Observe teams, map work and review tools/assistants | Process map; paid/configured/used inventory; baseline shortlist | Sponsor and process owners |
| 3–4 | Rank 10–20 opportunities and agree trials | Scored register; scope, data boundaries, owners and test plans | Supervisor |
| 5–7 | Run two or three controlled trials | Versioned examples, timing evidence, errors and staff feedback | Process owners and supervisor |
| 8–9 | Evaluate fit and commercial paths | Existing/general/specialist/tailored comparison; 1-year and 3-year costs | Sponsor with IT and finance |
| 10 | Assess a tailored solution | Documented recommendation, constraints and next step | Sponsor |
| 11 | Prepare rollout, training and safeguards | Staff guide, ownership register, staged roadmap | Process and support owners |
| 12 | Present and hand over | Decision paper, walkthrough, evidence pack, rerun instructions | Sponsor accepts handover |

If access or staff availability is limited, reduce the number or breadth of trials with the supervisor rather than weakening the evidence.

## Twelve-month adoption plan

**First 90 days:** validate workflow pain, assess existing tools, complete controlled trials and decide what merits further work.

**Months 3–6:** roll out only approved successful workflows to a small group; monitor usage, accuracy, review burden and support demand. Train staff using representative examples and failure cases.

**Months 6–12:** compare actual value with the decision paper, review vendor renewals and duplicated capabilities, expand only where there is an owner and a justified benefit. Retire unsuccessful workflows through an agreed process.

## Handover checklist

- A business owner accepts the purpose and success criteria.
- An approved location holds prompts, templates, fixtures and version history.
- Data fields, sources, permissions, retention and access are documented.
- A test set includes expected successes and deliberate failures.
- An operator can rerun, review, export and stop the workflow.
- Support ownership, review dates and incident escalation are clear.
- Costs include subscriptions, setup, training, support and exit effort.
- Failure and rollback steps preserve the current manual process.
- Training explains that fluent output can still be wrong.
- The final decision distinguishes measured evidence, assumptions and unresolved risks.
