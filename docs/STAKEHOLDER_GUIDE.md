# What the Opportunity Lab does and why

The proposal is to test whether everyday recruitment administration can become faster without reducing the quality of candidate or client service. This demonstration gives management a way to examine the workflow, question the assumptions and choose a practical next step.

## The business problem to validate

Recruitment work often involves taking information from a conversation or system and turning it into another useful format: a job advert, candidate update or client report. Repeating that work may consume time and introduce inconsistent wording. These are hypotheses to investigate at PRG, not findings from an internal audit.

The project asks whether approved templates, features already in use or a managed AI tool can help. It does not assume that every task needs AI. Missing-field checks and arithmetic are often better handled by ordinary rules.

## The proposed workflow

1. **A staff member chooses approved information.** For example, a hiring manager’s role brief.
2. **The tool prepares a draft and flags gaps.** It must not fill gaps by guessing.
3. **The reviewer inspects the evidence.** Each output can be compared with the fields that produced it.
4. **A person approves the result.** In this prototype that only enables a local download.
5. **The trial records quality and elapsed time.** Review and correction time count against the benefit.

In the Trial studio, the transformation uses fixed templates. The saved workspace adds SQL persistence, recorded reviews and trial measurements. The separate AI assistant can send an explicitly submitted question and selected synthetic draft to OpenAI when its server connection and billing are ready. In a real trial, PRG would decide whether an existing tool, managed AI platform, specialist product or tailored interface best supports those steps.

## Three demonstrations

**Brief to advert.** Start with a fictional BESS engineer brief. The draft retains supplied role facts. Switch to the wind example: salary is absent, so approval is blocked. This illustrates a useful habit: surface the missing fact instead of making the copy look complete.

**Candidate care.** A recorded status becomes a simple update. The second example has no contact permission, so the app prepares a hold instruction instead of a candidate message. In a real implementation, communication preferences must come from a trusted record and the responsible person must approve release.

**Client pulse.** A synthetic pipeline becomes a client progress brief with a blocker and next action. Counts represent one cumulative cohort. They are not added together, and later-stage counts cannot exceed earlier-stage counts. A real reporting workflow would first agree definitions and reporting dates.

## How to choose what to trial

The register contains 16 ideas spanning recruitment, candidate care, sales, marketing, operations, workforce and finance. The provisional score gives 35% weight to impact, 25% to feasibility, 20% to adoption and 20% to lower risk. Staff should challenge those judgments. An attractive score never makes a sensitive workflow automatically suitable.

Start by asking a process owner to show the work. Record actual volume, elapsed time, rework, system handovers and the cost of mistakes. Then choose two or three small trials with clear success criteria.

## The four commercial paths

| Path | Why choose it | What must be checked |
|---|---|---|
| Use existing tools | Could reduce duplication and fit staff habits | Paid features, configuration, usage, extra fees and quality |
| General AI platform | Could support several teams through approved templates | Data controls, review work, access and manual transfer steps |
| Specialist software | Could solve a focused gap better than general tools | Measured advantage, total cost, overlap and exit options |
| Tailored PRG solution | Could join a proven workflow that existing products do not support | Named owner, permissions, maintenance, integration and support |

The cost model includes setup, training, software and support. It shows one-year and three-year costs, along with capacity value after review time and adoption. Figures are working assumptions; comparable vendor quotations and internal costs would replace them.

## The decision requested after discovery

Agree the workflows, process owners, allowed data, measurement plan and stop rules for a controlled pilot. A successful pilot may justify a limited rollout. An unsuccessful pilot should produce a useful explanation and stop. There is no purchase, live integration or production deployment decision implied by this prototype.

## Ownership

The project sponsor selects priorities and accepts the final recommendation. A process owner verifies the workflow and output quality. IT/security and the relevant data owner approve access. A named support owner maintains anything adopted. The intern can coordinate discovery, tests, evidence and handover under supervision.
